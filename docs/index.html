Aletheia

Aletheia is a way for people and AI agents to search for new results without fooling themselves. It keeps a rough idea alive long enough to find the sound claim inside it. Then it checks that claim hard, with independent code, before anyone calls it new.

The name is the Greek word for truth as something uncovered. The method and its first field test were born together in one run. The field test has its own site: the Cave Expedition. Every claim on this site links to the ledger entry that backs it there.

00//The trap it answers

Plato's cave, in working form

Two ways to miss the truth.

A reviewer trained on finished papers grades a rough draft like a finished paper. It finds the first bug, files a clean rejection, and stops. Ask the same system to be open minded with no hard checks and it does the opposite. It praises word matches and decimal coincidences as breakthroughs.

Aletheia treats those two failures as separate dials, not one slider. Openness and rigor each go to full, in separate steps of one loop. The loop has a keeper who never leaves. When a test surprises anyone, the keeper goes back into the raw source with that surprise as a lens and looks again. Bugs get logged in full. Then the team states the strongest sound claim near the broken draft and tests that claim instead.

Checks run from a second angle with no shared code. A result that only holds in one set of coordinates, one unit, or one program is treated as a shadow of the setup, not a fact about the world. Every claim starts as known material. It earns a stronger label only after a full-text search of prior work.

01//Proof it changed the outcome

Same team, same sources, one instruction apart

First pass found nothing new. Second pass filled a ledger.

In November 2026 one team of agents worked the same raw transcripts twice. The first pass used normal review habits and closed with no new ground. One instruction from the lead kept the map maker inside the loop and asked the team to stay open before dismissing anything. The second pass produced a full audit ledger across maths and physics.

Side by sideFirst passSecond pass onward
Map makerOne static map, then parked as done.Stayed active. Re-entered raw sources after every surprise.
Rough draftsBugs listed. Files closed.Bugs logged. Sound claims beside them found and tested.
CheckerSaid yes or no.Supplied counterexamples and exact walls that told the team what to build next.
Yield0 results the team could stand behind as new.19 audited results in the expedition ledger. An outside check later confirmed the central sums it covered, kept one as new with medium confidence, one as a strong candidate, and moved the rest to local advances or known material under other names.

This was a before and after comparison, not a controlled trial. The second pass also ran longer. The full record, including the outside check that cut several claims down, sits in the Cave Expedition ledger. The cuts improved the method. They are why prior-art search now hunts by mechanism, not by name.

04//Same name, different thing

Read this before citing

Not the DeepMind prover.

Google DeepMind publishes a maths proving agent with the same name. The two projects share a word and nothing else. This Aletheia is an open method, a checking standard and a small program. It works with any model or with no model at all. The repo states the split in full near the top of its readme, with links to the other project.
