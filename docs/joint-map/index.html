Joint map

Aletheia did not come first on paper. It came out of a live run, the Cave Expedition, when one instruction mid-run changed how the same team worked the same sources. This page pairs each method rule with the ledger entry that shows it at work.

Facts on this page come from the case study in this repo, evidence/cave-expedition-case-study.md. Proof links go to the Cave Expedition ledger on the twin site.

01//Joint birth

Same team, same sources, one instruction apart

First pass closed files. Second pass filled a ledger.

The run worked two raw session transcripts plus a sweep of peer repos. The first pass used normal review habits. One instruction kept the map maker in the loop and told the team to stay open before dismissing anything. Work after that point produced the ledger.

  • #10 to #14Pass 1: static critique. The cartographer made one static map, flagged draft bugs, handed them on and parked as done. The verifier filed AUDIT-001 as falsified and AUDIT-003 as partial. Yield: 0 results the team could stand behind as N2. A sibling team on overlapping sources read the same way: new ground, still none.
  • #15 to #16The directive. The lead told cartography to stay actively involved, called the raw sessions a portal into discovery and asked for an open mind before any dismissal. That one instruction is the seed of Law 1 and Law 2.
  • #15 to #100Pass 2 onward: resonance. The keeper re-entered raw sources on each surprise. Bugs stayed logged in full while sound claims beside them were built and tested. Over 100 minutes and 86 board items, against 5 items in pass 1, the team labelled 10 results N2 by its own verifier, plus 1 at N1/N2 and 8 at N1, across 14 clean room check scripts.
  • Outside checkIndependent two pass check, cave-expedition#4. The outside check confirmed each central sum it covered. It kept 1 result at N2 with medium confidence and 1 as a strong candidate. It moved the rest to N1 or N0 after it found the same mechanisms under other fields names.
  • v2.0Gates learned from the cut. The downgrades drove Gate 9, mechanism first prior art, and Gate 10, the second pass advocate. Law 2b, the transformational steelman, came from the same round of outside review. The cuts made the method stricter.

This was a before and after record, not a controlled trial. The second pass ran longer and started from the first pass maps. Time, rounds and prior maps all differed. The case study states this limit in full.

04//Outside check

What it changed, in full

The cuts improved the method.

The two pass outside check in cave-expedition#4 confirmed each central sum it covered. It kept AUDIT-017, the F8 to E8 fold, at N2 with medium confidence, and AUDIT-009, the all-n Dn fold, as a strong N2 candidate. It moved the rest of the home N2 set to N1 or N0. In each moved case it found the same mechanism at work under another fields name.

Two early write ups needed fixes. The q identity is a diagonal similarity, not a Doob transform, since non-goal rows do not sum to 1. The Lindblad Schur rate is the zero frequency reduction, not the exact slow decay rate when the tidal rate is non-zero. One link, the Kigami fractal link, saw no independent check in the outside pass. Two check scripts, check_007 and check_008, later went stale against patched heads and need re-pinning. All 10 cross-repo outputs stay as open draft requests for human review. The Lean file stays tagged as uncompiled source.

Those findings rewrote this method. Prior art search now hunts by mechanism across at least two nearby fields under Gate 9. Every downgrade now gets a second pass advocate under Gate 10. Home labels still start at N0. A home verified tag means the claim cleared this protocol. It does not mean peer review cleared it.
